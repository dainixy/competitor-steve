The story in 60 seconds

Claude is a great builder and a bad designer — by default it produces the average of the internet: same fonts, purple gradients, everything equally loud. Real designers fix this with three moves, and we installed all three.

1 · Rules go in

Instruction files ("skills") now load automatically whenever Claude designs — taste rules from working designers, your two UX courses boiled down to 110 rules, and hard speed limits. Claude stops guessing.

2 · Eyes on the output

A reviewer opens the finished page in an invisible browser, screenshots it on phone / tablet / desktop, checks loading speed, and returns a fix list. Claude is a much better critic than creator — it just needs to see its own work.

3 · You judge

Never accept the first design. Ask for 3–5 versions, pick the best, iterate on that one. AI is cheap at making options and bad at knowing which is good. Your taste is the final filter — nobody has automated that away.

The one finding with a real measured test behind it: skills written as principles ("blur your eyes — can you still see what matters first?") beat skills written as pixel commands ("use exactly 16px") — 75% win rate in blind judging. Our whole setup follows that.

The DRT.FM look — rendered live

From your five answers: quiet luxury · dark-first · rose accent · serif headlines · Alexis-first landing. Everything below is real CSS from the brand bible, not a mockup.

The dark ramp — depth without shadows

Never pure black. Three surface steps + a hairline do all the depth work. Shadows are reserved for things that truly float (menus, dialogs).

One accent with a job description

Your site today runs two accents — purple #c084fc and rose. The purple is retired (purple gradients are the #1 "made by AI" tell). The rose stays, refined, and gets an exclusive contract:

Rose may appear as

  • THE pay/upgrade button (one per screen area)
  • the marker on the active nav item
  • a selected item's ring + faint tint
  • at most one word in a big headline

Rose is banned from

  • icons, links, borders, hovers
  • gradients and any decoration
  • meaning two different things
  • more than ~2% of any screen

Why so strict? Both competitors do exactly this — that's why their pay buttons are impossible to miss. Scarcity is what makes the color work.

Buttons — three tiers, instantly readable

money action · main action · everything else — never two rose buttons on one screen

Type — a quiet voice and a rare special voice

Satoshi (clean sans) does all interface work. Instrument Serif — the elegant serif you see in this page's headlines — appears only for big headlines and character names, so it stays special. Two small font files total, where OurDream ships eight (and pays for it in speed).

The character card recipe

Alexis 24
She remembers what you said last Tuesday — and she's been thinking about it.
  • The image is the card — no frame, no border, no shadow, no text block glued underneath.
  • Text sits on a smooth gradient (many tiny steps — if you can see where the gradient begins, it's wrong).
  • One identity line + one hook written to you, cut at two lines. No tag lists, no stat rows.
  • Hover reveals more content (a motion preview) — it never zooms or glows.

Motion & speed — restraint is the brand

Motion

Three speeds only (120 / 200 / 300ms), one easing curve. No scroll animations, no floating particles, no pulsing dots. Animation exists to give feedback, not to perform.

Hard speed limits

Page under 1.5MB · scripts under 300KB · loads in under 2.5s · nothing jumps while loading. These are blockers in every design review — because Google ranks on them.

What we learned from the competitors — with receipts

A robot browser measured both sites' actual code (~4,000 elements each). Their polish isn't magic — it's discipline. We copy the discipline, never the look.

Candy.AI — logged-out homepage

  • The landing page IS the product — real characters, working nav, stories rail. No "welcome to our website" hero.
  • Almost zero shadows anywhere — depth comes from lighter panels on darker background (our ramp does the same).
  • The sale banner is custom campaign art, not text slapped on a gradient — and it lives inside the content grid.
  • Status pills (LIVE, -70%) each own one color and one meaning — you scan them without reading.

Candy.AI — pricing

  • One screen, three cards, no comparison table. Only the recommended plan gets color — so the eye lands exactly where they want the click.
  • Under the buttons, the two anxieties answered before they're asked: "Charge shown as 'EverAI' on your bank statement" and "Cancel in settings." Trust text at the moment of doubt.
  • Benefits are a short icon list, not a 12-row checkmark matrix.

OurDream — landing grid (the discipline benchmark)

  • Count the pink: two buttons on the whole screen (Join Free, Upgrade). Everything else is gray — the photos supply all the color. That's the under-2% accent rule, live.
  • The search box teaches by example — a real query you could type, not "Search..."
  • The 75% sale card is exactly the same size and shape as a character card. Marketing never breaks the grid.
  • Under the hood: ~400 named design variables. Their consistency is mechanical, not heroic — that's what DRTFM-DESIGN.md gives us.
  • Their one weakness: 8+ font families loaded = slow. We ship 2 and beat them on speed.

OurDream — "no chats yet" screen

  • An empty screen is a doorway, not a dead end: one friendly sentence, real character photos fanned like polaroids, one button to the next step.
  • Compare with the default AI answer — a gray icon and "No data." Every DRT.FM empty state follows the doorway rule.

Your site today → what changes

drt.fm as it looks now — good bones: dark, grid landing, Alexis trailer up top

  • Keep: the dark canvas, the character grid landing, Alexis leading the page (your five answers formalized exactly this).
  • Change: retire the purple — one rose accent with the job contract above. Purple + rose together is the classic AI-slop palette.
  • Change: headlines move to the serif (like this page), UI text to one clean sans — instant "designed, not generated" feel.

Your two courses, distilled

All PDFs, the 603-page book, and 43 transcribed video hours → one playbook (12 topics) + 110 one-line rules that Claude now reads automatically. A few favorites, so you know the flavor:

  • Decide in this order: what's the goal → how is it organized → how does it behave → how does it look. Visual polish is always last.
  • The squint test: blur your eyes at any screen. If you can't tell what matters first, the screen is broken — nothing is accentuated when everything shouts.
  • Space tells the truth: things in a group sit close; groups sit far apart. Whitespace separates better than lines and boxes.
  • Hierarchy from weight, not size: bolder and dimmer beats bigger and bigger.
  • When there's no data, familiarity wins: copy the pattern users already know (Jakob's Law) instead of inventing a "better" one.
  • Standard is fast: save originality for the one thing that makes you different; everything else should be boringly familiar — which is also the cheapest to load.
  • Never letter-space lowercase text. (A hard rule from the typography module — one of those things you can't unsee.)

How to use it — the daily loop

1

Give the chat the brand bible

In the DRT.FM prototype chat: copy DRTFM-DESIGN.md into that repo's root, then say: "Read DRTFM-DESIGN.md fully before building anything." This is the only manual step — everything else loads by itself.

2

Build with a named direction

Never say "make it modern and clean" (that's how you get the average). Say what and for whom: "Build the pricing page per DESIGN.md — quiet luxury, one rose CTA, Alexis presence at top." Screenshots of things you like are welcome fuel.

3

Clean it

Type /design-deslop — strips the AI fingerprints. For a deeper pass: /baseline-ui, then /fixing-accessibility, then /fixing-motion-performance.

4

Let it see itself

Say: "Run a live design review on http://localhost:3000." You get screenshots at three screen sizes, a speed score, and a fix list sorted Blocker → Nitpick. Fix the blockers, run again.

5

Judge like an owner

Unsure between directions? "Give me 4 visual variants of this screen as plain HTML files." Open them, pick one, kill the rest. Then the squint test: one focal point, clear reading order? Ship. When in doubt — remove one thing, don't add.
