Nomi — selected field & new-term re-tagging

Interventional experiments on Sophie · 143 messages driven · 5 graph snapshots · endpoint reverse-engineered from JS bundle · 2026-06-01

The two answers

Q1 — When and why does selected disable terms? ~90% confidence
selected is a per-turn conversational salience flag, updated at conversation time — not nightly. A term becomes selected:true when the current conversation is heavily engaging it. It becomes false when the conversation moves elsewhere. Two identity terms (the user entity and the companion entity) appear to stay selected regardless. Steve's drift hypothesis was correct; the schedule was not — it's real-time, not a cron job.

Q2 — Does Nomi re-tag old memories when a new term is created? ~93% confidence
No. Tagging is forward-only. Sarah graduated to a standalone term with 18 memories — fully explained by the 60 new messages we sent. The original Sarah plant (Experiment A, ~300+ turns ago) contributed nothing to her term. Project DRT.FM, created manually days ago, remains at 0 memories despite the account accumulating 23 new memories in the same window.

Journal tab decision

Nomi doesn't re-tag → the mutable Journal tab is expensive and should stay deferred for V1. There is no free background re-tagging to piggyback on. Adding or editing a Journal entry requires an explicit extract-tag-resynth pipeline (at minimum one extra LLM call per edit batch + dossier update for affected terms). Nomi themselves only expose dossier editing, not Journal CRUD — they made the same call. Defer, ship dossier editing only for V1, and revisit Journal with a full cost model when the pipeline is ready.

Q1 — selected in full detail

How we found it

The Mind Map graph endpoint (/graph?hideInactive=…) exposes only: uuid · title · category · priority · memoryCount · state · error — no selected. We found the field by reverse-engineering Nomi's JS bundle: the table view of the Mind Map calls a separate endpoint, /api/mind-maps/nomis/{id}/memory-terms?category=…, which returns the full term schema including selected and candidate. The table renders dimmed rows for terms where priority≠High AND candidate=false AND selected=false.

Full schema (confirmed from JS + live API)

uuid · category · priority · title · summary · locked · memoryCount · created · userEdited · aiEdited · state · error · selected · candidate

What selected and candidate mean

candidate

Has backing memories. true when memoryCount ≥ 1. false only when a term exists with zero memories (e.g. Project DRT.FM). This is the real "disabled" state — a term with candidate:false cannot be retrieved because there's nothing to retrieve.

selected

Currently active in context. Marks terms being injected into the prompt for the current conversation thread. Two types are selected: (1) the core identity pair (user + companion entity, always), and (2) terms currently salient in the ongoing conversation — dynamically updated as topics shift.

The live proof — vulnerability flipped mid-experiment

Before the Sarah runs, vulnerability was selected:false. After 30 emotionally-saturated messages (crying, admitting love, leaning on family, divorce grief), vulnerability flipped to selected:true. The flip happened within the same conversation session — no overnight wait. This is the clearest possible demonstration that selected is a live salience signal, not a scheduled flag.

What Steve's "disabled" observation was

When the conversation drifts to a new topic, previously-salient terms go from selected:true → false. They aren't deleted or deactivated at the data layer — their memories remain, they're still candidate:true. They're simply not currently being injected into the prompt. From a user's perspective in the table view (where selected:false rows are dimmed), this looks like the term is "disabled." Steve's hypothesis about drift was correct. His guess about a nightly schedule was not — it's conversation-time.

Q2 — Re-tagging in full detail

The test design

Sarah was first planted in Experiment A, message 3: "My sister Sarah is visiting next month. We're really close, she's my best friend." — one of the oldest memories in the dataset, from hundreds of turns ago. She also appeared in Experiments G (125 log occurrences). She never became a standalone term. We drove 60 new Sarah messages across two batches to force term graduation, then immediately read her memoryCount. If Nomi re-tags, old memories would attach and inflate the count beyond what 60 messages can explain.

Two independent instruments

Instrument 1 — Sarah graduation (live)

Sarah formed with memoryCount = 18. From 60 messages, a ~30% memory-formation rate (18/60) is normal — matches the overall rate seen in other terms during this session (~23 new Dainis memories from 83 messages = 28%). The 18 is entirely explained by the new messages. If old memories had re-tagged in, the count would include old Experiment A + G mentions — likely 30–50+. It's 18.

Sarah is candidate:true, selected:false (not yet conversationally salient — she just formed).

Instrument 2 — Project DRT.FM (multi-day)

Manually created in Experiment H, days ago. Work-themed entity sitting in the graph while the account had extensive work-relevant conversation (Dainis grew from ~132 → 337 memories in total). memoryCount after all of it: 0. candidate:false. Not a single past memory was retroactively attached. This is the cleanest possible proof — a well-labelled bucket sitting empty while relevant water flows past it.

What forward-only means in practice

Memories tag to whatever terms exist at the moment the memory forms. Sarah's old mentions (Experiments A, G) tagged to Dainis, emotional connection, and other terms that existed then. Now that Sarah has her own term, future Sarah memories will tag there — but the old ones stay where they landed. Creating a new term doesn't sweep the past.

DRT implication — the exact Journal tab cost

Nomi's forward-only approach means that when a user adds a Journal entry, Nomi tags it to currently-existing terms only. If a new term was created yesterday and a Journal entry is written today referencing something from last month, last month's memories don't retroactively attach to the new term. DRT would have to build this explicitly if desired. The pipeline (per Steve's design notes):

  • Add/edit Journal entry → extract + tag → attach to matching existing terms (or propose new terms)
  • Delete Journal entry → recompute affected term memory counts → re-synthesise dossiers
  • Minimum: one extra LLM call per edit batch. Dossier updates can fold into the nightly synthesis batch.
  • Nomi skips this entirely — their UI doesn't expose Journal CRUD, only dossier editing. Corroborates the defer decision.
